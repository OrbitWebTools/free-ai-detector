1. AI Text Detector: Perplexity Clichés, Burstiness & Vocabulary Diversity

Large language models such as ChatGPT (GPT-4o / GPT-5), Google Gemini AI, and Anthropic Claude generate text token-by-token by choosing statistically probable continuations. As a result, raw AI essays and articles exhibit low perplexity (predictable vocabulary like "delve into", "pivotal role", "multifaceted paradigm", and "in today's fast-paced world") and flat burstiness (multiple consecutive sentences clustering tightly around 19–25 words). Our AI text checker breaks your essay into individual sentences and highlights high-risk, mixed, and human-like lines so you can inspect exact stylistic triggers.

2. AI Image Detector: Binary EXIF, C2PA Content Credentials & Diffusion Forensics

Authentic camera photographs captured on iPhone, Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel, Canon, Nikon, or Sony hardware typically embed structured EXIF metadata (camera manufacturer, focal length, aperture, exposure time, ISO). By contrast, raw generations from Midjourney, OpenAI DALL-E, Stable Diffusion (Automatic1111 / ComfyUI), Adobe Firefly, and Google Imagen frequently contain PNG tEXt/iTXt generation prompts or cryptographic C2PA Content Credentials (trainedAlgorithmicMedia). Our browser scanner reads the file's raw binary header and tail locally, checks standard diffusion dimensions (such as 1024×1024 or 1344×768), and computes a canvas edge-noise gradient index—all without uploading your photo anywhere.

3. AI Code Detector: Spotting ChatGPT & Copilot Boilerplate

Whether you are a computer science instructor reviewing assignments or a developer auditing pull requests, AI-generated code leaves recognizable patterns: sequential tutorial comments (# Step 1:, # Step 2:), formal docstrings on trivial 5-line helpers, generic demo footers (# Example usage with sample_data), and unusually high comment-to-code ratios. Our AI Code Detector evaluates these signals locally so proprietary repositories never leave your device.

Academic & Publishing Workflow: Google Docs, APA/MLA Citations & Plagiarism

AI Content Detection vs. Plagiarism (Content Similarity Detection)

A common misconception among students writing a research paper or essay is treating AI detection and plagiarism as the same check. Plagiarism detection compares your text against published journals, websites, and student repositories to find copied passages. AI content detection evaluates statistical writing patterns—perplexity, burstiness, and formulaic transitions—to estimate whether an LLM drafted the text. A paper can have 0% plagiarism and still trigger an AI detector if the phrasing is robotic, or 0% AI and still be plagiarized if sources are not cited in APA style or MLA Handbook format.

How to Protect Your Authentic Work in Google Docs

Why AI Detectors Produce False Positives (And How to Read Scores Honestly)

Independent academic benchmarks consistently prove that no AI detector—free or paid—is 100% infallible. Responsible interpretation requires watching for four known edge cases:

  • Short Snippets Under 50 Words: Two or three sentences do not provide enough variance to separate human writing from AI reliably.
  • Non-Native English Writers: Multilingual students who master structured formal English frequently use clean textbook transitions that overlap with LLM probability distributions.
  • Heavily Grammar-Edited Drafts: Accepting every automated rewrite suggestion in Grammarly or QuillBot can strip away idiosyncratic human phrasing and lower burstiness.
  • Stripped Social Media Image Metadata: Downloading an image from WhatsApp, Instagram, or X/Twitter removes original EXIF and C2PA headers; in those cases, treat pixel-smoothness and dimension scores as supporting clues rather than definitive proof.
